Assigning is how you tell a line how much it can spend. Rollover decides what happens to the money that's left, or missing, when the period ends.

## How Available is worked out

For every line and period:

**Available** = money carried over from the last period + **Assigned** + **Activity**

**Activity** is negative when you spend, so spending lowers **Available**. The [side panel](/budgeting/side-panel/#breakdown) shows this sum for any line under **Breakdown**.

## Assign money to a line

<Steps>

1. Click the amount in the line's **Assigned** column. A window opens with the line's name at the top.
2. Enter the amount under **Assigned**.
3. Under **Amount applies to**, choose **This period only** or **From now on**.
4. Press Enter or click **Save**.

</Steps>

## This period only or from now on

Every line has a usual amount that repeats, and any period can make an exception.

- **From now on** sets the line's usual amount, its baseline. It applies to the period you're viewing and every period after it, until a later period sets a different **From now on** amount. Earlier periods keep what they had.
- **This period only** is a one-off. Only the period you're viewing uses it, and the next period goes back to the usual amount. The line shows an **override** badge in that period.

For example, you set Groceries to 400 **From now on** in March. April, May and every month after start with 400. In May you're hosting guests, so you set 500 **This period only**. June is back to 400.

The window starts on **From now on**, unless the period already has a one-off amount. Then it starts on **This period only**, so a quick change doesn't overwrite the usual amount by accident.

### Go back to the usual amount

When a period has a one-off amount, the window shows **Use baseline** under **Amount applies to**. Click it to remove the one-off, and the period uses the line's usual amount again.

### Assign ahead

Use the arrows next to the period at the top of the budget, or click the period to pick one from the list, to open a later period. You can assign money up to 12 periods ahead. The current period is marked **Current** in the list.

<Aside type="note" title="Another way to assign">
**Budget X** on a [goal](/budgeting/goals/#see-what-a-goal-needs) adds money on top of what the line already has, for this period only.
</Aside>

## Rollover

Rollover decides what a line brings into a new period from the one before. There are three rules:

| Rule | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Carry surplus** (the default) | Money left in a line moves to the next period. Overspending does not. |
| **Carry surplus and deficit** | Both leftovers and overspending move to the next period. |
| **Start fresh each period** | Every line starts at zero, whatever happened before. |

Here's what a Groceries line with 400 assigned every month starts April with:

| March ended with | Carry surplus | Carry surplus and deficit | Start fresh each period |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 left | 450 | 450 | 400 |
| 50 overspent | 400 | 350 | 400 |

A line using **Carry surplus and deficit** shows a **carries deficit** badge.

### Choose a rule for the whole budget

You pick the rule when you [create the budget](/budgeting/start-budgeting/#create-your-budget), under **Rollover**. To change it later:

<Steps>

1. Click **Budget settings** at the top right of the budget.
2. Under **Rollover default**, pick a rule. It saves right away.

</Steps>

Every line without its own rule follows the budget's rule, in every period. Changing it recalculates those lines, past periods included.

### Give a line its own rule

<Steps>

1. Click the line's **Assigned** amount in the period where the new rule should start.
2. Pick a rule under **Rollover**. The hint reminds you it applies from this period on.
3. Click **Save**.

</Steps>

The line keeps that rule in every later period, until you pick another one.

<Aside type="tip" title="The rule decides what a period brings in">
A period's rule decides what it takes over from the period before. For example, if you switch a line to **Start fresh each period** in April, April starts without March's leftovers.
</Aside>

### Where money that doesn't roll over goes

Money that isn't carried simply isn't counted any more. The next period starts from the new **Assigned** amount.
