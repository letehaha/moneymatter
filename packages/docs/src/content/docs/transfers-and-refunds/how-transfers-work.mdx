A transfer is money moving between your own accounts, for example from your checking account to your savings. You didn't spend or earn anything, so MoneyMatter keeps transfers out of your income and expense totals. This page covers the kinds of transfer, how to record one, and what happens to it afterwards.

## Kinds of transfer

MoneyMatter has a few kinds of transfer. They differ in where the money goes, how many transactions get recorded, and what opens when you click one in the transaction list.

| Kind | What's recorded | How you create it | What opens when you click it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Between accounts** | Two transactions: an expense on one account, an income on the other | **Transfer** tab, with **Account** picked in **Where the money goes** | **Edit transaction** |
| **Out of wallet** | One transaction on your account | **Transfer** tab, with **Out of wallet** as **From account** or **To account** | **Edit transaction** |
| **To portfolio** | One transaction on your account. The portfolio side is a cash entry in the portfolio. | **Portfolio** in **Where the money goes**, **Transfer to portfolio** on an account's page, or **Transfer** on a portfolio's page | **Portfolio-linked transaction**, a read-only view |
| **To loan** (a loan payment) | Two transactions: an expense on your account, an income on the loan | **Loan** in **Where the money goes**, or **Record payment** and **Link previous payments** on the loan's page | The **Edit loan payment** dialog |
| **To venture** | Your existing transaction, linked to a venture deal | Linking the transaction to an event on a venture deal | **Venture-linked transaction**, a read-only view |
| **To a shared account** | Two transactions, one on each owner's account | **Transfer** tab, with the shared account as **To account** | **Edit transaction** |

- **Between accounts.** Money moves from one of your MoneyMatter accounts to another. The two transactions are paired and act as one transfer. See [Create a transfer between your accounts](#create-a-transfer-between-your-accounts).
- **Out of wallet.** Money moves to or from somewhere you don't track in MoneyMatter, for example a savings account at another bank that you haven't added. There's no second account to put the other side on, so it's one transaction: an expense when money leaves, an income when money arrives. See [Out of wallet transfers](#out-of-wallet-transfers).
- **Portfolio, loan and venture transfers** are managed from their own pages. See [Transfers to a portfolio, loan or venture](#transfers-to-a-portfolio-loan-or-venture).
- **To a shared account.** If another person shares an account with you with **Can edit** or **Can manage** access, for example through a [household](/settings/household-and-sharing/), you can transfer from one of your accounts to theirs. See [Transfers to a shared account](#transfers-to-a-shared-account).

To find each kind in the transaction list, use the **Transfer kinds** filter. See [Transfers in the transaction list](#transfers-in-the-transaction-list).

## Create a transfer between your accounts

<Steps>

1. Click **Add transaction** at the top of the app (on a phone, tap **Add**). The **Add transaction** dialog opens.
2. Select the **Transfer** tab at the top of the dialog.
3. In **Amount**, enter how much left the source account.
4. In **From account**, pick the account the money left.
5. In the **Where the money goes** panel, keep **Account** selected and pick the destination in **To account**.
6. If the two accounts use different currencies, enter how much arrived in **Target amount**. See [Same currency or different currencies](#same-currency-or-different-currencies).
7. Check **Datetime**. You can also set **Payment type**, **Note** and **Tags** under **More options**, which is the right-hand column on a computer and a button at the bottom of the dialog on a phone.
8. Click **Create**.

</Steps>

If you click **Add transaction** while an account's page is open, **From account** is already set to that account.

A few fields you know from expenses and incomes aren't there. **Category**, **Payee** and the split option don't apply to transfers, because moving money between your accounts isn't spending on anything. Transfers are saved without a category.

### Same currency or different currencies

**When both accounts use the same currency**, both sides of the transfer always get the **Amount** value. **Target amount** fills in automatically, and whatever you type there is ignored. If you later change the amount, both sides change together.

**When the currencies differ**, you type both amounts yourself: what left in **Amount**, and what arrived in **Target amount**. **Target amount** becomes required. MoneyMatter doesn't look up or suggest an exchange rate here. The rate is whatever your two amounts imply, which is usually what you want, because it matches what your bank or exchange actually charged.

For example, you move 500 USD from Checking to a Travel card that holds EUR, and 460 EUR arrives. Enter 500 in **Amount** and 460 in **Target amount**. Checking goes down by 500 USD and the Travel card goes up by 460 EUR.

### What's shared between the two sides

The second transaction copies the date, note and payment type from the one you entered, and the two stay in sync when you edit them later. **Tags** are the exception: they're saved only on the **From account** side (the account the money left), not on both.

## Out of wallet transfers

Use **Out of wallet** when money leaves your tracked accounts or arrives from outside them without being spent or earned. Typical cases are moving savings to an account you don't track in MoneyMatter, or receiving money back from one. The app describes these as transfers "to and from accounts you don't track".

Why not record it as an expense or income? Because it would inflate your spending or your income, when really the money only moved somewhere you aren't tracking.

**Out of wallet** is the first option in both **From account** and **To account**.

- **Money leaving:** pick your account in **From account** and **Out of wallet** in **To account**. Enter the amount in **Amount**. **Target amount** is turned off. This creates one expense on your account.
- **Money arriving:** pick **Out of wallet** in **From account** and your account in **To account**. Enter the amount in **Target amount**. **Amount** is turned off. This creates one income on your account.

<Screenshot
  src="/screenshots/transfers-and-refunds/out-of-wallet-option.png"
  alt="The To account dropdown open on the Transfer tab, with Out of wallet listed first above the user's accounts and loans."
  hint="Add transaction, Transfer tab, pick 'Main Checking' in From account, then open the To account dropdown. Crop to the dropdown so 'Out of wallet' is visible at the top of the list with accounts below it."
/>

In the transaction list, an Out of wallet transfer shows **Out of wallet** where the other account's name would be.

To turn an Out of wallet transfer back into an ordinary expense or income, open it, select the **Expense** or **Income** tab, pick a category and click **Edit** (the save button at the bottom of the dialog).

If you later start tracking the other account, you can join the two records into a regular transfer. See [Link existing transactions](/transfers-and-refunds/link-existing-transactions/).

<Aside type="caution" title="Don't just swap Out of wallet for an account">
To turn an Out of wallet transfer into a transfer between two of your accounts, don't open it and pick one of your accounts in place of **Out of wallet** in **From account** or **To account**. MoneyMatter doesn't create the other side when you do that, so nothing is recorded on the other account and its balance doesn't change. Instead:

- If the other side is already recorded, use **Link the other side**, as described in [Link existing transactions](/transfers-and-refunds/link-existing-transactions/).
- If it isn't, first turn the transaction into an ordinary expense or income as described above and save it. Then open it again and [convert it into a transfer](#turn-an-expense-or-income-into-a-transfer).
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Good to know">
Some imports, such as the [Microsoft Money import](/import/other-import-sources/#microsoft-money), bring in a transfer as Out of wallet when its other account isn't part of the import. You can link these to the other side afterwards.
</Aside>

### Other transactions recorded as Out of wallet

MoneyMatter also uses Out of wallet for a few records it creates itself. They show up under the **Out of wallet** filter too.

- **Balance adjustments.** When you use **Adjust balance** on an account, the difference is saved as an Out of wallet transaction, so it changes the balance without counting as income or spending. See [Adjust a balance](/accounts/manage-accounts/#adjust-a-balance).
- **Vehicle value overrides.** When you [override a vehicle's value](/accounts/vehicles/#override-the-value), the change is saved as an Out of wallet transaction on the vehicle. Clicking it opens a read-only **Vehicle adjustment** view with a button to the vehicle's page, where you change or delete the override.
- **Household transfers that lost their other side.** See [Transfers to a shared account](#transfers-to-a-shared-account).

## Transfers to a portfolio, loan or venture

These transfers have their own dialogs and pages. The **Edit**, **Unlink** and **Delete** steps further down this page don't apply to them.

### To a portfolio

A transfer to or from an [investment portfolio](/investments/portfolios/) is one transaction on your account. The money on the portfolio's side is a cash entry in the portfolio, not a transaction.

- **Create it** with **Portfolio** in **Where the money goes** on the **Transfer** tab, with the **Transfer to portfolio** button on an account's page, or with **Transfer** on a portfolio's page. You can also turn an existing expense or income into one: open it, select the **Transfer** tab and pick **Portfolio**.
- **Click it** in the list to open the read-only **Portfolio-linked transaction** view. It shows the portfolio and the amount, with an **Unlink from portfolio** button.
- **Unlinking** gives the transaction back its original details and reverses its effect on the portfolio's cash.
- **Deleting** works only after you unlink it, or from the portfolio's **Activity** tab.

<Screenshot
  src="/screenshots/transfers-and-refunds/portfolio-linked-transaction.png"
  alt="The Portfolio-linked transaction view, saying the transaction is linked as a deposit to a portfolio, with the amount and the Unlink from portfolio button."
  hint="Create a portfolio named 'Brokerage' (USD). On the seeded Everyday Checking account page, click the Transfer to portfolio button (⇄ icon in the header) and transfer 300 USD to 'Brokerage'. Open /transactions and click that transaction. Crop to the dialog."
/>

When you turn an older expense into a portfolio transfer, you can tick **Portfolio balance already includes this money**. The transfer then counts toward the portfolio's contributions without adding cash to it, which is what you want when you set up the portfolio with its current balance. See [Portfolios](/investments/portfolios/#cash).

### To a loan

A loan payment is a pair of transactions, like a transfer between accounts: an expense on the account you paid from, and an income on the loan that lowers what you owe.

- **Create it** with **Loan** in **Where the money goes** on the **Transfer** tab, or by picking a loan as **To account**. An existing expense can become a loan payment the same way; an income can't. On the loan's page you can also use [**Record payment**](/accounts/loans/#record-a-payment), or **Link previous payments** to turn expenses you already have into loan payments.
- **Click it** in the list to open the **Edit loan payment** dialog. There you change the account, amount and date, and click **Save**.
- **Unlink** keeps the expense as an ordinary expense and takes it off the loan. The loan balance is recalculated.
- **Delete** moves both transactions to Trash and gives the loan its balance back.

Unlike other transfers, the money leaving your account counts as spending. See [Loans](/accounts/loans/#edit-unlink-or-delete-a-payment).

### To a venture

A [venture](/investments/venture/) transfer is a transaction you linked to an event on a venture deal, such as the money you sent for an investment or received from an exit. It stays one transaction.

- **Create it** on the deal's page, by linking existing transactions when you add an event.
- **Click it** in the list to open the read-only **Venture-linked transaction** view, with an **Open venture** button that takes you to the deal.
- **To undo the link**, delete the event on the deal, or the whole deal. Leave **Also delete the linked transaction(s)** unticked to keep the transaction: it goes back to how it was before you linked it.
- **Editing or deleting** the transaction isn't possible while it's linked. See [Venture](/investments/venture/#what-linking-does-to-a-transaction).

## Transfers to a shared account

If someone shares an account with you with **Can edit** or **Can manage** access, for example through a [household](/settings/household-and-sharing/), you can transfer money from one of your own accounts to it. Use the **Transfer** tab as usual and pick their account in **To account**.

- It's recorded as a pair, like a transfer between your own accounts. Each side counts in its owner's base currency.
- The money has to leave one of your own accounts. You can't start a transfer from an account someone shares with you.

If the household ends, because someone [leaves or is removed](/settings/household-and-sharing/#leave-or-remove-someone), or if one of the two accounts is deleted, the transfer is split. Each side becomes an Out of wallet transaction on its own account, and its note gains a line saying where the money went or came from, such as "Transfer to @name • Savings". Balances don't change.

## How transfers affect balances

Each side of a transfer changes its own account, just like any expense or income. A 500 USD transfer from Checking to Savings takes 500 USD off Checking and adds 500 USD to Savings. With different currencies, each account moves by its own amount in its own currency.

An Out of wallet transfer only changes the one account it's on.

- **Portfolio transfers** change the account's balance and the portfolio's cash, unless you ticked **Portfolio balance already includes this money**.
- **Loan payments** lower what you owe on the loan. A payment dated before the loan's **Balance as of** date is kept as history and doesn't change it. See [Loans](/accounts/loans/).
- **Venture transfers** don't change any balance when you link them. The transaction already moved the money on its account, and the deal's value comes from its events.

## How transfers affect stats

Transfers are left out of income and expense totals: cash flow, spending by category, and the other reports and widgets that add up what you earned and spent. The exception is a payment to a loan you track: the money leaving your account counts as spending. Moving 1,000 from Checking to Savings doesn't mean you spent 1,000 and earned 1,000, so counting it would make both your spending and your income look bigger than they were.

Out of wallet transfers, portfolio transfers and venture transfers are also left out of income and expense totals. Because they do change an account's balance, they still show up in that account's balance and balance history. On the **Cash flow** report, money moved into a portfolio or a venture shows as a savings destination under **Where the money went**. See [Analytics reports](/stats/analytics-reports/#where-the-money-went).

For the full picture of what's counted where, see [How stats are calculated](/stats/how-stats-are-calculated/).

## Transfers in the transaction list

A transfer shows both account names with an arrow between them. On **All transactions** it appears once, not twice. On an account's own page, you see the side that belongs to that account.

To find transfers, open the **Filters** menu above the list and add:

- **Transfers**, to show **All** transactions, **Exclude** transfers, or show **Only** transfers.
- **Transfer kinds**, to narrow down to **Between accounts**, **Out of wallet**, **To portfolio** or **To venture**.

There's no **Transfer kinds** option for loan payments. **Transfers** set to **Only** includes them, and **Exclude** hides them.

See [Search and filter](/transactions/search-and-filter/#available-filters) for the other filters.

## Edit a transfer

This section, [Unlink a transfer](#unlink-a-transfer) and [Delete a transfer](#delete-a-transfer) are about transfers between accounts and Out of wallet transfers. Loan payments, portfolio transfers and venture transfers open their own views instead. See [Transfers to a portfolio, loan or venture](#transfers-to-a-portfolio-loan-or-venture).

Click either side of a transfer in the list to open **Edit transaction**. The form always shows the source as **From account** and **Amount**, and the destination as **To account** and **Target amount**, whichever row you clicked.

Change what you need and click **Edit**. Changes to the amounts, the accounts, the date, the note and the payment type are applied to both sides.

The **Account** / **Portfolio** / **Loan** choice in **Where the money goes** is locked on an existing transfer. To send the money somewhere of a different kind, [unlink the transfer](#unlink-a-transfer) first.

### Turn an expense or income into a transfer

If you recorded a transfer as an ordinary expense or income, you can convert it.

<Steps>

1. Open the transaction and select the **Transfer** tab.
2. Fill in the account that's missing. Converting an expense keeps its account as **From account**. Converting an income puts its account in **To account** and leaves **From account** empty for you to pick.
3. Check the amounts, then click **Edit**.

</Steps>

When you convert an expense, MoneyMatter creates the other side on the account you picked.

When you convert an income, the transaction you opened becomes the expense on the **From account** you picked, and a new income is created on its original account. Its tags, payee and attachments stay with the expense.

<Aside type="caution" title="Splits are removed">
Transfers can't be split. If the transaction was [split across categories](/transactions/split-a-transaction/), converting it to a transfer deletes the splits, and there's no confirmation step.
</Aside>

If the other side is already recorded, for example because both came from bank imports, don't convert. [Link the two instead](/transfers-and-refunds/link-existing-transactions/), otherwise you'll end up with a duplicate.

### Turn a transfer back into an expense or income

Selecting the **Expense** or **Income** tab on a transfer, picking a category and clicking **Edit** turns the **From account** side (the expense) into an ordinary expense or income, whichever of the two rows you clicked. If one side came from a bank connection, that side is the one that changes. **The other side, on the other account, isn't deleted.** It stays on its account as a separate, ordinary transaction, keeps affecting that account's balance, and starts counting in your income and expense stats. Delete it or link it to something else if you don't want it.

If you want to keep both sides as ordinary transactions, [unlinking](#unlink-a-transfer) is the simpler option.

## Unlink a transfer

Unlinking turns a transfer back into two separate transactions, without deleting anything.

<Steps>

1. Click the transfer in the list to open **Edit transaction**.
2. In the **Where the money goes** panel, click **Unlink transactions**.

</Steps>

The dialog closes straight away. Both transactions keep their amounts, dates and accounts, but they're now an ordinary expense and an ordinary income, so they now count in your income and expense stats.

<Screenshot
  src="/screenshots/transfers-and-refunds/edit-transfer-unlink.png"
  alt="The Edit transaction dialog for a transfer from Main Checking to Savings, with the Unlink transactions button inside the Where the money goes panel."
  hint="Click an existing Main Checking to Savings transfer in /transactions. Crop the Edit transaction dialog to the From account field and the Where the money goes panel, including the Unlink transactions button."
/>

A transfer you added with the **Transfer** tab has no category, so after unlinking both of its transactions are uncategorized. Give them categories if you're keeping them as spending and income.

## Delete a transfer

Deleting either side of a transfer moves **both** transactions to Trash, because together they're one transfer. The confirmation dialog, **Move to Trash?**, doesn't mention the other side, so keep this in mind before you click **Delete**.

Both stay in Trash for 30 days. To bring them back, click **Trash** on the **Transactions** page, pick the transfer and click **Restore**. After 30 days they're deleted for good. See [Trash](/transactions/trash/).

If you only want to get rid of one side, [unlink the transfer](#unlink-a-transfer) first, then delete the one you don't need.

## Transfers with bank-connected accounts <Badge text="Plus" variant="tip" />

Transactions that come from a [bank connection](/import/bank-connections/) are controlled by the bank: you can't change their amount, date or account, you can't switch them between expense and income, and you can't delete them. You can still turn them into a transfer. Bank connections are part of the Plus plan, see [Plans and billing](/settings/plans-and-billing/).

Bank-connected accounts don't appear in **From account** or **To account** when you add a new transfer. Start from the bank's transaction instead. The bank's side stays fixed: an imported expense stays as **From account**, an imported income stays as **To account**.

<Steps>

1. Open the transaction the bank imported and select the **Transfer** tab.
2. If the bank's transaction is an expense, pick the destination in **To account** and check **Target amount**. If it's an income, pick the source in **From account** and check **Amount**. Enter the amount yourself if the currencies differ.
3. Click **Edit**.

</Steps>

MoneyMatter creates the other side as a manual transaction on the account you picked.

<Aside type="caution" title="Don't edit a transfer that arrived in a bank-connected account">
When the bank's transaction is the income side of a transfer, don't edit and save that transfer, not even to change the note. This applies whether you converted it or linked it. Saving can turn the other side, the expense on the **From account**, into an income, so that account's balance goes up instead of down. To change such a transfer, [unlink it](#unlink-a-transfer), edit the transactions separately, then join them again with [Link the other side](/transfers-and-refunds/link-existing-transactions/).
</Aside>

If the other side was also imported, for example you moved money between two banks that are both connected, [link the two transactions](/transfers-and-refunds/link-existing-transactions/) instead.

A transfer that involves a bank-connected account can't be deleted from MoneyMatter, and the **Delete** button doesn't appear for it. You can still [unlink it](#unlink-a-transfer). The exception is a transaction you added by hand before you linked that account to your bank: it stays editable and can be deleted.

## Good to know

- **Transfers can't be dated in the future.** The date picker stops at today. Transfers also can't be planned, so the **Transfer** tab is turned off while you're adding a planned transaction.
- **Attachments are added after saving.** You can't attach a file while creating a transfer. Open the saved transfer to add one.
- **Refunds and transfers don't mix.** A transfer can't be linked as a refund, and the **Link refund** button doesn't appear on the **Transfer** tab. See [Refunds](/transfers-and-refunds/refunds/).
